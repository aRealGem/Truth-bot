The core assertion—that there were 11,888 murders—is stated as a bare numerical fact without qualification. Evidence shows Trump repeatedly cited this figure (E1, E2), but FactCheck.org clarifies (E2) that it refers specifically to 'noncitizens convicted of murder who were not being detained,' not murders that occurred in a given year or period. E4 and E5 establish that no national data directly counts crimes by immigration status by year, and treating a database total as annual murders conflates different measures. The figure appears to be a cumulative or database count of convicted noncitizens, not an annual murder count or a claim about murders 'committed' in real time. Stating '11,888 murders' without this critical context—that it refers to a subset of convicted noncitizens not in detention, not a count of murders that occurred—exaggerates and distorts the underlying data, creating a false impression of scale and immediacy.
