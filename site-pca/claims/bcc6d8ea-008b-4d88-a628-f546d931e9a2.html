The core assertion—that new laws have been passed in multiple states both to suppress voting AND to subvert entire elections—is partially supported but materially overstated. Evidence confirms that 19 states passed laws making voting harder (E5, suppression component TRUE) and that bills introducing partisan interference in election processes were introduced and some passed (E4, E6, subversion component REAL but narrower in scope). However, E6 states legislators 'introduced and passed' such bills without quantifying how many actually became law, and E4 notes these are 'a new trend' with 'momentum' but does not establish that subversion laws were passed 'in state after state' with the same breadth as suppression laws. The claim conflates introduced bills with enacted laws and treats the two categories (suppression and subversion) as equally widespread, when evidence shows suppression laws are documented in 19 states while subversion laws are described as emerging trend with less systematic passage. The underlying phenomena are real, but the phrasing 'in state after state, new laws have been passed...to subvert entire elections' overstates the prevalence and certainty of subversion-specific legislation relative to what evidence establishes.
