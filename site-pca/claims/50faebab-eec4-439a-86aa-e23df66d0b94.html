The core assertion is that NATO countries 'are paying five as opposed to not paying three.' The evidence confirms NATO agreed to a 5% GDP defense spending target (E1, E2, E5, E6), but this target is for 2035, not current payment as of Feb 2026. The claim uses present tense ('are paying five') to describe a future commitment, creating a false impression of immediate compliance. Additionally, the comparison 'five as opposed to not paying three' is incoherent—the prior target was 2%, not 3%, and countries were not 'not paying' it but rather failing to meet it. The claim conflates a future pledge with present reality, misleadingly suggesting NATO members are currently paying 5% when they have only committed to reach that by 2035.
