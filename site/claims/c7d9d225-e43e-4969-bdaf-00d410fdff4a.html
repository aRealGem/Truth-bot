Model reasoning — Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7

The 11,888 (and earlier 13,099) figure is drawn from an ICE letter showing noncitizens convicted of homicide on ICE's non-detained docket — but this data spans roughly 40 years and is not limited to those admitted under Biden. Trump, who was campaigning at the time, distorted the data, falsely claiming that then Vice President Kamala Harris 'let in 13,099 convicted murderers,' and more recently has repeatedly used the figure '11,888 murderers,' though he appears to be citing the same data. DHS told FactCheck.org that the figures weren't just for recent immigrants, but included people who entered the country over the last 40 years or more. While it is correct that 13,099 noncitizens on the ICE docket have been convicted of homicide, this data spans 40 years, and does not cover only the Biden-Harris administration. Nor can it be interpreted as having happened only 'in the last short period of time.' Furthermore, the figures include individuals who are in jails and prisons serving criminal sentences. Many are serving sentences in state/federal prisons, not 'roaming free,' and many entered legally.

❌ False
Model reasoning — Google Gemini 2.5 Pro

There is no official data from federal agencies like the FBI or the Bureau of Justice Statistics to support the claim that 11,888 murders have been committed by undocumented immigrants who entered the U.S. under the Biden administration. The federal government does not systematically track crime statistics by the immigration status of the perpetrator in a way that would allow for such a specific calculation. Furthermore, multiple studies analyzing crime data, particularly from Texas which does track arrests by immigration status, have found that undocumented immigrants have significantly lower homicide arrest and conviction rates compared to native-born U.S. citizens. For example, a Cato Institute analysis of Texas data from 2013-2022 found native-born Americans committed homicide at a 36% higher rate than undocumented immigrants.
