The Big Beautiful Bill did not actually eliminate taxes on Social Security benefits. Per the IRS, the law created a new $6,000 additional deduction for individuals 65 and older (effective 2025-2028), phasing out above $75,000 ($150,000 joint) MAGI. TurboTax explicitly notes the OBBB 'didn't eliminate taxes on Social Security benefits' and 'didn't change the way Social Security benefits are taxed at all,' but for some seniors the deduction reduces taxable income enough that no federal tax is owed. The SSA and White House say nearly 90% of seniors will pay no federal income tax on their benefits as a result. So Trump's framing as 'no tax on Social Security' is technically inaccurate—the underlying tax structure is unchanged—though the deduction effectively zeroes out tax liability for many seniors.

The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," signed into law in July 2025, did not eliminate taxes on Social Security benefits for all seniors. Instead, it introduced a new, temporary standard deduction for individuals aged 65 and older, available from tax years 2025 through 2028. This deduction can reduce a senior's taxable income, which may in turn reduce or eliminate the federal income tax on their Social Security benefits, particularly for those with moderate incomes.
