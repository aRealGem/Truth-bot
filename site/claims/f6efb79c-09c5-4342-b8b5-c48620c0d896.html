Model reasoning — Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7

The 2-percentage-point figure refers only to the high end of CBO's estimate for the impact on Q4 2025 annualized GDP growth in an 8-week shutdown scenario, not the cost to overall GDP. CBO estimated that depending on its length, the government shutdown will reduce annualized real GDP growth in [Q4 2025] by 1.0 to 2.0 percentage points. The actual six-week shutdown reduced Q4 GDP growth by about 1.5 points, not 2.0. Crucially, most of that loss is recovered: CBO estimated that by the first quarter of FY2027, the cumulative effects of real GDP of a six-week shutdown would be a loss of $11 billion—less than 1% of GDP. So the '2 points on GDP' framing conflates a one-quarter annualized growth-rate dip (largely temporary) with a permanent loss of 2% of GDP, which is far larger than the actual permanent impact (around $11B, well under 0.1% of GDP).

Model reasoning — Google Gemini 2.5 Pro

The claim that a government shutdown cost the U.S. two points on GDP is a significant overstatement. Reports from the Congressional Budget Office (CBO), the non-partisan agency that analyzes economic data for Congress, show that shutdowns have a much smaller effect. For example, the CBO estimated the five-week shutdown of 2018-2019 reduced quarterly real GDP by 0.1% in the fourth quarter of 2018 and 0.2% in the first quarter of 2019. This is a fraction of the "two points" (a two percentage point reduction) claimed. While a CBO analysis of a hypothetical eight-week shutdown projected a 2.0 percentage point reduction in the *annualized quarterly growth rate*, this is a temporary effect on a single quarter's growth, not a two-point loss from the annual GDP, and most of that lost activity is recovered in the subsequent quarter.
