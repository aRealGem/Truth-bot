George Washington created the predecessor of the Purple Heart, the Badge of Military Merit, by general orders at his Newburgh, New York headquarters on August 7, 1782, while serving as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army (not as president). On August 7, 1782, General George Washington established the "Badge of Military Merit" to honor enlisted soldiers for "any singularly meritorious Action." This award, in the shape of a purple heart made of cloth or silk, is considered America's first military decoration and the direct predecessor to the modern Purple Heart. The award fell into disuse after the Revolutionary War but was officially revived and renamed the Purple Heart on the bicentennial of Washington's birth, February 22, 1932, by order of Army Chief of Staff General Douglas MacArthur. The revived medal explicitly honors Washington's memory and original military achievements.
