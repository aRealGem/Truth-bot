anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — 🟢 Mostly True High

Trump's claim about the murder rate specifically (as opposed to the overall crime rate) is largely supported by the Council on Criminal Justice's 2025 analysis, which projects that once FBI finalizes the 2025 data, the national homicide rate will drop to about 4.0 per 100,000 residents — the lowest rate recorded in law enforcement or public health data going back to 1900, eclipsing the previous FBI-reported low of 4.4 per 100,000 in 2014, and representing the largest single-year percentage drop on record. However, important nuance is missing: the 2025 figure remains a projection, not a final FBI number, and comparing modern data to pre-1960 figures requires combining not-fully-comparable sources (1930–1959 FBI data and 1900–1929 public health homicide data, which uses a broader category than 'murder'). Analyst Jeff Asher notes 1954–1956 may have been comparably low, but methodology then was less reliable. The claim is directionally accurate for the murder rate specifically, though Trump has sometimes conflated it with the overall crime rate.
