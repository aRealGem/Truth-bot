anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — 🟢 Mostly True High

Trump's claim that the murder rate saw its single largest decline in recorded history in 2025 is supported by preliminary data from multiple independent sources, though official FBI data won't be released until late 2026. According to AH Datalytics' Real-Time Crime Index, which compiles data from 570 law enforcement agencies, murders fell approximately 20% from 2024 to 2025 — a roughly 20 percent drop in murder in 2025 would be by far the largest decline ever recorded, eclipsing the decline in 2024 — currently pegged at -15 percent by the FBI. The Council on Criminal Justice found homicide declined by 21% from 2024 to 2025 in its study cities, and the council and Asher agreed that the 2025 drop of about 20% is likely to become the largest one-year decline ever recorded. Preliminary FBI data supports the trend: Preliminary data the FBI made public earlier this year showed that homicides across the country fell 18% between September 2024 and August 2025. The FBI data also showed an overall 9% decline in violent crime during the same time period and a 12% reduction in property crime. However, the claim requires nuance: while Trump officials are taking credit, the decline in murder is part of a larger trend dating back several years. Homicides surged during the pandemic, but the drop in crime continues a trend that began in 2023, accelerated in 2024, and likely became historic in 2025. Additionally, the drop in 2025 was the largest one year drop ever recorded for both murder and homicide. The CDC's homicide data back to 1900 puts the 2025 decline as the largest ever at 15 percent, supplanting a 14 percent drop in 2002 and 13ish percent drops in 1935 and 1945.
