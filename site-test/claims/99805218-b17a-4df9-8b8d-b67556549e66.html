On March 5, 2026, Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey reported the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 6.00%, which according to Freddie Mac was the lowest level in over 3 years, down from 6.16% a month earlier. Freddie Mac's chief economist Sam Khater explicitly framed this as "the lowest level since September of 2022" — approximately 3.5 years, not four. NerdWallet similarly noted rates went below 6% in late February 2026, and that rates hadn't started with a five since September 2022. So the claim is directionally correct — rates were at a multi-year low near the date in question — but overstates the time span. The accurate framing is "lowest in about 3 to 3.5 years" (since September 2022), not four years. Going back four years from March 2026 would be March 2022, when rates were around 3–4% (well below 6%), so the four-year characterization is factually wrong. Additionally, context matters: Bankrate reported the 30-year rate averaged 6.18% for the first two months of 2026, compared with above 7% a year earlier, confirming a meaningful decline but not a four-year record.

The claim is false. According to the evidence provided with the claim, mortgage rates in early 2026 were around 6.18% to 6.30%, with one source specifying that a rate of 6.30% was a four-week low, not a four-year low. Historical data from Freddie Mac, the primary source for U.S. mortgage rates, confirms this. For example, in March 2022, the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was between 3.76% and 4.67%, and the average for the entire year was 5.34%. These figures are significantly lower than the rates reported for March 2026, which were around 6.00% to 6.38%.
