anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — ⚠️ Misleading High

Trump's two-part claim is partly supportable but substantially misleading. The first part—that core inflation fell to its lowest level in more than five years—has some support: CNBC reported that core CPI was up 2.5% year-over-year in January 2026, described as the lowest level since April 2021, which is roughly five years. However, the second part—that core inflation ran at 1.7% in the last three months of 2025—is not supported by any Tier 1 BLS data. BLS's December 2025 CPI release shows the all items index rose 2.7% over 12 months and the all items less food and energy (core) rose 0.2% in December alone, not an annualized 1.7% over the quarter. Multiple fact-checkers note that BLS core inflation ran 2.6% in both November and December 2025 and 2.5% in January 2026, with October 2025 data missing due to the government shutdown, and analysts could not identify the source of Trump's 1.7% figure. One Hill analysis suggests Trump's economists may have divided the 2.6% two-month figure by three months (treating the missing October as zero) to arrive at 1.7%. NBC News and Medill rated the overall claim false, while ABC News rated it 'yes and no.' Additionally, core inflation was already declining under Biden (it was 3.3% in January 2025 when Trump took office), so crediting the drop entirely to Trump's administration is misleading; inflation even spiked to 3% in September 2025 amid tariffs before cooling.

xai / grok-4 — ❌ False High

According to primary data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), the 12-month core inflation rate (all items less food and energy) was 2.6% in November 2025 and December 2025, not 1.7% as claimed by Trump for the last three months of 2025. Furthermore, this 2.6% rate is not the lowest in more than five years; lower rates were recorded in early 2021, such as 1.3% in February 2021, which is within the five-year period preceding late 2025. The rate continued to decline to 2.5% in January 2026, but still not the lowest in over five years, as rates below 2.0% occurred in 2020 and early 2021. This contradicts the claim using official BLS monthly data.
