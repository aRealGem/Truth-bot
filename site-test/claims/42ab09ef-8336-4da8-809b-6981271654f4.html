Inflation in January 2025, when Trump took office, was not at record levels. When Trump took office in January 2025, the annualized rate of inflation was 3% based on the CPI — far from the 9.1% rate in June 2022 under Biden, which was the highest 12-month increase since November 1981, and far from the worst inflation in U.S. history, which was the 23.7% rate for the 12 months ending in June 1920. The year-over-year inflation rate in January 2025 was 3.0%, was 2.9% in December 2024 (Biden's last full month), and hadn't been close to 5% since early 2023. So Trump did not 'inherit a nation with inflation at record levels' — the rate was elevated but well below recent peaks and far below historical records.

Model reasoning — OpenAI GPT 5.4

This claim is contradicted by BLS data. Around February 2025—the point Trump referenced as “12 months prior”—inflation was nowhere near a U.S. record: BLS reported consumer prices were up 2.8% from February 2024 to February 2025. By contrast, BLS says the CPI peak in the recent inflation surge was 9.1% in June 2022, the largest 12-month increase since November 1981, and historical BLS material shows even higher inflation in earlier U.S. history, including a 23.7% 12-month increase from June 1919 to June 1920. So saying he inherited inflation at “record levels” in February 2025 is false for both the immediate data point and the longer historical comparison.
