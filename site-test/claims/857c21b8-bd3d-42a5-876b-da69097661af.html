Trump's claim that core inflation was 'down to 1.7%' in the last three months of 2025 relies on a non-standard framing. The most commonly cited core inflation figures from the Bureau of Labor Statistics show the core inflation rate was 2.6% in November 2025 and in December 2025, and data for October 2025 is unavailable because of the federal government shutdown. The Treasury Department itself reported that over the twelve months through September 2025, the core inflation rate was 3.0 percent, and annual core inflation has ranged between 2.8 percent and 3.1 percent so far this year. The 1.7% figure appears to come from a three-month annualized calculation: using the standard three-month annualized measure of seasonally adjusted core CPI (less food and energy), the rate was about 1.7 percent at the end of 2025, comparing the December 2025 core CPI index level (331.814) to the September 2025 level (330.418) and annualizing the three-month change. While that specific calculation is mathematically valid, critics note the framing is misleading: Trump cited the last three months of 2025, which would have included October, but the Bureau of Labor Statistics did not release a figure for October because of the government shutdown, and core inflation was 2.6 percent in both November and December and 2.5 percent in January — down from 3.3 percent in January 2025. An alternative quarterly-average approach yields a roughly 2.2 percent annualized rate for fourth quarter 2025. Trump cherry-picked the most favorable three-month annualized metric while the headline year-over-year core CPI remained at 2.6%.
