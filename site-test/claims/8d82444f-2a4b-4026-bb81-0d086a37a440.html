🟢 Mostly True
Model reasoning — Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7

The core CPI claim is technically supportable for the January 2026 reading. The CPI rose 2.4% in January from a year earlier, down from 2.7% in December. Excluding food and energy, the core CPI was up 2.5%, the lowest level since April 2021. Core inflation was 2.5% in January 2026, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, the lowest since 1.6% in March 2021 – nearly five years ago. So core inflation is at roughly a five-year low (just under five years from April 2021 to January 2026). However, fact-checkers note nuance: while it's true that inflation has eased in recent months, federal data does not show as dramatic a decline as Trump has claimed. Trump's accompanying claim that core inflation was 1.7% in the last three months of 2025 is disputed: core inflation was 2.6 percent in both November and December and 2.5 percent in January — down from 3.3 percent in January 2025. Trump cited the last three months of 2025, which would have included October, but BLS did not release a figure for October because of the government shutdown.

Model reasoning — OpenAI GPT 5.4

Primary BLS data support the broad claim. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that core CPI ('all items less food and energy') was 2.5% year over year in January 2026 and again 2.5% in February 2026. That is the lowest core inflation rate in more than five years, because by early 2021 core CPI had already moved above that level and then remained higher through the 2021-2025 inflation surge. So within roughly 12 months of taking office on January 20, 2025, core inflation had fallen to a five-plus-year low. The nuance is that saying his administration 'drove' it down assigns full causal credit to the administration for a trend the data alone do not prove was caused solely by White House policy.

This was a decrease from the 3.1% rate reported by the BLS for the 12 months ending in February 2025, confirming that core inflation was driven down during the first year of the second Trump administration. The 2.5% rate was widely reported as the lowest since March 2021, which is 4 years and 11 months prior to the speech, just shy of the five-year mark.
