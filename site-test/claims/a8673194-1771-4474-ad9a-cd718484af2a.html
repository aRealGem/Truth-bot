According to Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey (the standard primary source for U.S. mortgage rates), the 30-year fixed rate was 6.00% for the week ending March 5, 2026 — essentially the rate in effect as of March 4. This was down only modestly from 6.15% at the end of 2025 and 6.16% on January 8, 2026, a decline of roughly 15–16 basis points over about two months. Freddie Mac noted the rate had just dropped below 6% on Feb. 26, 2026 for the first time in about three and a half years, and described it as 'holding steady' at 6% the week of March 5 — not 'falling fast.' Year-over-year, rates were down from roughly 6.85–6.93% a year earlier, a more meaningful but still gradual decline. Moreover, rates reversed course immediately after: 6.11% on March 12, 6.22% on March 19, 6.38% on March 26, and 6.46% on April 2, 2026. Characterizing this as rates 'falling fast' substantially overstates a modest, gradual decline that was already flattening and about to reverse.
