Model reasoning — Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7

The claim references Trump's Feb. 24, 2026 State of the Union, in which he recapped his prior joint-session address (March 4, 2025) and characterized the economy he inherited as having 'inflation at record levels.' This is false. According to BLS data cited by FactCheck.org, when Trump took office in January 2025, the annualized rate of inflation was 3%, far from the 9.1% rate in June 2022 under Biden (the highest 12-month increase since November 1981), and the worst inflation in U.S. history was after World War I when CPI rose 23.7% for the 12 months ending June 1920. FactCheck.org explicitly rated as false Trump's claim that he inherited 'a stagnant economy' with 'inflation at record levels,' noting the economy was not stagnant under Biden. In his earlier March 4, 2025 joint address, Trump said 'we inherited from the last administration an economic catastrophe and an inflation nightmare... We suffered the worst inflation in 48 years, but perhaps even in the history of our country, they're not sure' — a claim CBS rated as only partially true since the June 2022 inflation reading of 9% was the highest since November 1981, but inflation was higher for longer in the 1970s and 1980s, peaking in April 1980 at 14.6%. Crucially, inflation rates were declining before Trump returned to the White House in January 2025; core inflation climbed as high as 6.6% in 2022 but was down to 3.2% in December 2024. Tier 1 Treasury data confirms annual core inflation included a 3.3% rating in January from when President Trump assumed office — nowhere near record levels.

Model reasoning — Google Gemini 2.5 Flash

Donald Trump was inaugurated for his second term as the 47th U.S. President on January 20, 2025, meaning he did 'inherit' the nation around February 2025. However, his claim that inflation was at 'record levels' at that time is false. In January 2025, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 3.0% year-over-year, and in February 2025, it increased by 2.8% year-over-year. These figures were significantly lower than the peak inflation rates seen between 2021 and 2022, when headline CPI rose above 9%. In fact, the 3.1% increase in core CPI (excluding food and energy) in February 2025 was reported as the 'smallest 12-month advance since April 2021'. This indicates that inflation was moderating and stabilizing around 3-4% in early 2025, rather than being at record levels.
