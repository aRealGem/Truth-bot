anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — ❌ False High

Trump claimed at his March 4, 2026 State of the Union that gasoline was below $2.30 a gallon in most states and as low as $1.99 in some places. Fact-checkers directly contradicted this. According to PolitiFact, looking at statewide averages, not one state had an average below $2.30 per gallon, according to AAA data. PolitiFact also noted that a company tracking gasoline prices said no gas station was charging $1.98 per gallon when Trump made similar claims recently. EIA data confirms prices were substantially higher: the average monthly price per gallon was $2.91 in February 2026 and $2.81 in January 2026, with the last time monthly gas prices being higher was September 2023. By the time of the SOTU, prices were already rising sharply due to Middle East conflict; on March 5, 2026, AAA reported the national average jumped nearly 27 cents in one week to $3.25, as conflict in the Middle East sent crude oil prices higher. PBS NewsHour reporting indicated Arkansas, Kansas and Mississippi were states with average prices at or below $2.50 a gallon — still well above $1.99. While a handful of individual stations may have had promotional low prices, no state average approached $1.99, and GasBuddy found no stations at that level.

gemini / gemini-2.5-pro — ⚠️ Misleading High

The claim that gasoline was as low as $1.99 a gallon in some places is technically possible for a few individual gas stations, but it creates a false impression of the overall market. Around the time of the address, data from AAA and the Bureau of Transportation Statistics show that no state had an average gas price below $3.00, and the national average was rapidly increasing. On March 5, 2026, the national average was $3.25, and by March 11, the cheapest state average, Kansas, was $3.01. The lowest price found at any specific station on March 5 was $2.19 in Alabama. Therefore, while a handful of stations might have had prices near $1.99, the broader claim that prices were "below $2.30 a gallon in most states" was false, making the overall statement misleading.
