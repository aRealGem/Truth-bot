anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 🟢 Mostly True

Trump's claim that gasoline is 'as low as $1.99 a gallon in some U.S. places' is narrowly accurate if interpreted as referring to a small number of individual gas stations. Reporting from around the time of the claim found that a tiny handful of stations were indeed selling gas at or below $1.99 per gallon — PBS NewsHour identified stations in Iowa (23), Kansas (3), and Texas (2) charging $1.99 a gallon, and CNN reported there were some individual gas stations selling gas for under $2 per gallon, but fewer than 100 stations across the country below $2 out of roughly 150,000 stations GasBuddy tracks. However, the companion framing Trump typically used — that gas was below $2.30 in 'most states' — is false: while gas prices had dropped from a national peak of $5.02 in June 2022 to $2.95 according to AAA, they were not below $2.30 in most states; only one state, Oklahoma, had an average gas price around $2.30 as of Feb. 24, and the national average price of a gallon of regular gasoline was still over $3, with no state having an average price below $2.60. EIA data confirms that by March 2026, prices were actually rising sharply due to Middle East conflict; on March 30, the U.S. average retail gasoline price was $3.99 per gallon, the highest in real terms in over two years. So the $1.99 figure reflected rare outlier stations, not a representative price point.
