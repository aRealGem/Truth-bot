anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — 🟢 Mostly True Medium

The claim aligns with projections from the Council on Criminal Justice (CCJ), which found that with homicide down 21%, the 2025 homicide rate may reach the lowest level ever recorded in law enforcement or public health data going back to 1900. CCJ projects that once nationwide FBI data is released, the 2025 homicide rate will drop to about 4.0 per 100,000 residents — which would be the lowest rate ever recorded going back to 1900 and would mark the largest single-year percentage drop in the homicide rate on record. However, important caveats undermine the certainty: the national murder rate is likely to land near 4.0 per 100,000 people once the FBI releases finalized 2025 data later this year, meaning final nationwide FBI figures are not yet available. Additionally, experts expect that when the final 2025 murder rate is released, it likely will be the lowest in at least 65 years, but whether it is the lowest in 125 years is disputed because experts say data prior to 1960 is not comparable to later data. The problem with the 125-year record claim is that it requires including not-fully-comparable 1930 to 1959 FBI data and 1900 to 1929 public health data, which counted homicides — a broader category than murders and non-negligent homicides because it also includes killings considered justifiable. Analyst Jeff Asher notes the safest thing we can say is that the 2025 murder rate was likely the lowest ever recorded, though three years — 1954 to 1956 — stand out as possibly equal or lower, but the FBI's methodology those years wasn't nearly as reliable as it was a few years later. PolitiFact rated a similar Trump statement 'Half True.'

Preliminary data and analyses from credible sources indicate the U.S. homicide rate dropped to approximately 4.0 per 100,000 in 2025, marking the largest single-year decline on record and one of the lowest rates since reliable records began around 1900.
