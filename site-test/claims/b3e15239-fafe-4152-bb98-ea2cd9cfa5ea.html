According to Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, the primary government-affiliated source for mortgage rate data, the 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 6.00% as of March 5, 2026, slightly up from 5.98% the prior week, compared to 6.63% a year earlier. Freddie Mac's chief economist Sam Khater characterized this level as 'hovering near their lowest level since 2022,' not explicitly a four-year low. CNBC similarly reported that mortgage rates dropped to the lowest level since 2022. Since 30-year fixed rates first crossed above 6% around September 2022 and stayed there, rates at 6.00% in early March 2026 represent roughly a 3- to 3.5-year low, not a full four-year low (four years prior, in March 2022, rates were around 4%, well below 6%). Trump's framing is directionally correct — rates were indeed at multi-year lows near the time cited — but 'lowest in four years' slightly overstates the actual benchmark, which major sources consistently described as the lowest since 2022.
