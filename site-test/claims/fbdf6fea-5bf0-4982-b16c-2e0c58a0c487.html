Model reasoning — Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7

DHS has used the 56% figure: since President Trump took office, fentanyl trafficking at the southern border is down by 56% compared to the same period in 2024. However, this figure refers to fentanyl seizures (an enforcement output), not 'flow' across the border. WOLA's analysis of CBP data finds CBP's U.S.-Mexico border fentanyl seizures fell to 11,486 pounds in 2025, the fewest since 2021. That is a remarkable 46 percent drop from 2024 and a 57 percent drop from 2023. Whether declining seizures reflect reduced flow or reduced enforcement detection is contested—the trend predates Trump's second term, with seizures already dropping sharply since 2023. The 56% number is roughly accurate for the cited timeframe, but characterizing it as the 'flow' going down (versus seizures) is the standard misinterpretation; seizures are an imperfect proxy for actual flow.
