anthropic
📊 Exaggerated
Model reasoning — Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7

Similar to the tips claim, this is overstated. Per IRS guidance, the Big Beautiful Bill created a deduction (not an elimination) for qualified overtime compensation effective 2025-2028. The maximum annual deduction is $12,500 ($25,000 for joint filers), phasing out for taxpayers with MAGI over $150,000 ($300,000 joint). Only the 'half' portion of FLSA-required time-and-a-half is deductible — not the entire overtime payment — and only overtime required under federal FLSA qualifies (not state-required or contractual overtime). The deduction applies only to federal income tax, not payroll taxes. So overtime pay is not entirely tax-free; it remains subject to FICA, state income taxes (in most states), and federal income tax beyond the capped deductible portion.

Model reasoning — Google Gemini 2.5 Pro

The 2025 tax law, referred to as the "Big Beautiful Bill," did not eliminate taxes on overtime pay. The legislation created a new, temporary tax deduction for overtime compensation. Summaries of the law explain that this provision, which allows both itemizers and non-itemizers to deduct overtime pay, is effective for tax years 2025 through 2028. A tax deduction lowers a taxpayer's taxable income but does not entirely eliminate the tax on that income, making the claim of elimination an overstatement of the bill's actual effect.
