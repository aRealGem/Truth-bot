anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — 🟢 Mostly True High

Trump's claim that the murder rate saw its single largest decline in recorded history in 2025 is supported by the best available preliminary data, though the official FBI national data will not be released until later in 2026. The Council on Criminal Justice's January 2026 report found homicides fell 21% from 2024 to 2025 in 35 major cities, and projected that if similar declines appear in the FBI's nationwide data, the 2025 homicide rate would be approximately 4.0 per 100,000 residents—the lowest since at least 1900 and the largest single-year percentage drop on record. Independent analyst Jeff Asher of AH Datalytics reached similar conclusions using the Real-Time Crime Index, finding murder down roughly 20% and calling it 'by far the largest decline ever recorded.' However, important nuances qualify the claim: (1) the FBI's official 2025 national data has not yet been released, so these remain projections based on city-level samples and preliminary law enforcement data; (2) the decline is part of a multi-year trend that began in 2022-2023, before Trump took office; (3) CCJ itself cautioned that its findings do not prove any specific policy caused the declines; and (4) PolitiFact notes that direct comparisons to data before 1960 are disputed because older data used different definitions and coverage. So while the claim that 2025 will show the largest single-year percentage drop in the murder rate on record is well-supported, attributing it solely to Trump's first year is misleading since the trend predates his second term.

Multiple analyses, including from the Council on Criminal Justice, Jeff Asher, and preliminary FBI statements, confirm that the U.S. homicide rate (often used interchangeably with murder rate in these contexts) declined by approximately 15-21% in 2025 compared to 2024.
