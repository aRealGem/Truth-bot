By the primary metric typically cited—illegal border crossings—the claim is well-supported by Tier 1 government data. U.S. Border Patrol encounters with migrants crossing into the United States from Mexico have fallen to their lowest level in more than 50 years. The Border Patrol recorded 237,538 encounters with migrants at the U.S.-Mexico border in fiscal year 2025, down from more than 1.5 million in fiscal 2024 and a record of more than 2.2 million in fiscal 2022. The 2025 total was the lowest in any fiscal year since 1970. CBP data confirms dramatic month-over-month declines: In March 2025, USBP apprehended 7,181 illegal aliens crossing the southwest border between ports of entry, a 95% decrease from March 2024 when USBP apprehended 137,473 aliens. CBP Commissioner Rodney Scott stated border crossings in November 2025 were even less than October's historic low, making for the lowest encounter numbers ever to start a fiscal year, with seven straight months of zero releases. U.S. Border Patrol recorded the lowest number of apprehensions along the southwest border ever in the first quarter of a fiscal year (21,815 in FY2026), 95% lower than the first quarter average under the Biden administration. However, 'most secure border in American history' is somewhat subjective; historical comparisons are limited because monthly encounter data only goes back about 25 years in comparable form, and the dramatic decline follows policy changes by both Biden and Trump administrations, and Mexico's increased enforcement reportedly has played a significant role in reducing migrant flows. Also, during Trump's first term, fact-checkers noted border apprehensions were actually higher than under Obama, so the 'history' framing has previously been overstated.
