anthropic / claude-opus-4-7 — 🟢 Mostly True High

Trump's claim is rooted in a January 2026 Council on Criminal Justice (CCJ) year-end report. With homicide down 21%, the 2025 homicide rate may reach the lowest level ever recorded in law enforcement or public health data going back to 1900, according to CCJ's year-end analysis. CCJ projected that when nationwide FBI data is reported, homicides in 2025 will likely drop to about 4.0 per 100,000 residents — the lowest rate ever recorded in law enforcement or public health data going back to 1900, and the largest single-year percentage drop on record. However, the claim omits important caveats. First, the CCJ analysis is based on study cities (homicide declined 21% from 2024 to 2025 in these cities), with the nationwide conclusion being a projection pending FBI data release later in 2026. Second, reliable historical comparison is limited: analyst Jeff Asher notes the US did not formally start systemically measuring murder as a crime until 1930, and there aren't reliable national estimates until around 1960, so it's not correct to say anything definitive about the US murder rate prior to 1900. Additionally, the US homicide rate in the early 1900s is officially reported at around 1 per 100,000, but researchers estimate the actual rate was closer to 7 or 8 per 100,000 — data that CCJ and other scholars consider more reliable than the official data. The FBI's most recent official data shows murder and non-negligent manslaughter had a 2024 estimated nationwide decrease of 14.9% compared to 2023, supporting the broader downward trend. The White House picked up on the CCJ report without the estimative caveats, but murder did fall a lot last year and at the fastest rate ever recorded to likely the lowest murder rate ever recorded. So Trump's directional claim is accurate, but stated with more certainty than the underlying preliminary, city-based data justifies.
